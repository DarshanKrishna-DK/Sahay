The afternoon nobody gets back

Sourcing is a phone job, and the phone job is yours.

You open IndiaMART, scroll fifty listings, guess at a shortlist, then spend an afternoon having the same six-question conversation over and over. Most of those calls go nowhere. The ones that don't are not written down anywhere.

01

The listed price is not the price

B2B marketplace rates are indicative. The real number only comes out of a phone call - usually in a regional language, often in a dialect.

02

You are the bottleneck

A solo owner cannot make twenty calls and still run the shop. So you call three, take the best of three, and never learn what the other seventeen would have said.

03

Nothing gets remembered

Last quarter's quoted rate lives in somebody's head. Next quarter you negotiate from zero, with no leverage and no record.

The core - agentic procurement

Tell it what you need. It does the whole cycle.

One sentence in, five negotiated deals out. Everything below happens without you touching a phone.

1

You say what you need

“500 cotton t-shirts, 180 GSM, under ₹180 a piece, delivered in Tiruppur.” Typed or spoken, in any of eleven Indian languages. Sahay turns it into a structured requirement and asks only about what is genuinely missing - never a form.

2

It finds and scores the vendors

Sahay searches IndiaMART and comparable B2B sources, then scores every candidate on price against your target, locality, whether their MOQ is realistic for your quantity, stock, and whether they can actually be reached. A rock-bottom rate 900 km away with a minimum order five times your quantity is not a deal - and it gets ranked like one.

3

It calls them and negotiates

The agent opens by stating it is an automated assistant calling for you - always, non-negotiable. Then it works through rate, minimum order, lead time, delivery and payment terms, and makes one polite push toward your target. It never commits to an order and never quotes one vendor's price to another.

4

Five deals, ranked by landed cost

Not the headline rate - the number that actually leaves your bank account, freight and quantity included, with lead time and payment terms beside it. Every term links to the transcript sentence where the vendor said it, so you can check any figure before you trust it.

5

You pick. It raises the order.

On your confirmation - and only then - Sahay generates a proper purchase order, sends it to the vendor, and raises a payment link. It tracks the order from sent through paid to delivered. Sahay never moves money on its own.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask.

Does it really call my suppliers?

It really holds the negotiation - a genuine two-way spoken conversation, in the vendor's own language, with a full transcript at the end. Voice and telephone carriage are two separate things, so during pilot the conversation runs over voice messaging while the telephone line is connected. The agent, the script, the transcript and the extracted quote are identical either way.

Will vendors know they are talking to a machine?

Yes, in the first sentence, every time. Disclosure is hard-coded and not configurable. The agent identifies itself as an automated assistant and names the buyer it is calling for. If a vendor asks not to be contacted again, that is recorded permanently and they are never dialled again.

Can it place an order on IndiaMART for me?

No - and nothing can, because those marketplaces have no public ordering API. They are a discovery surface. Sahay does what real B2B procurement does: on your confirmation it generates a proper purchase order with a reference number and full terms, sends it to the vendor, raises a payment link, and tracks the order through to delivery.

What stops it inventing a price?

Every commercial term is extracted from a recorded transcript and carries a confidence score, and the audit log stores the exact sentence the vendor said it in. If a vendor never gave a rate, the rate stays empty - Sahay will tell you it could not get one rather than fill the gap. There is no sample-data fallback anywhere in the system: a broken connector surfaces as an error, because a business decision made on an invented number is worse than no answer.

Who can see my negotiated rates?

Only you. What a vendor agreed to on your call is commercially sensitive, so every business is fully isolated - one workspace's vendor list, quotes and order history are never visible to another. Cross-tenant rate benchmarking exists only at the cluster tier, only aggregated and anonymised, and only if you opt in.

Do I need to read English?

No. Hindi, Kannada, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Bengali, Gujarati, Malayalam, Punjabi, Odia and English are all first-class. Speak to it in yours and it answers in yours, by voice.
